Resource residency & provisioning boundary

Who owns the Azure resources, where they live, and what the runtime is allowed to create when a session starts.

Owns Decision 30 Customer-owned Sandbox Group v1 · 1 group per app/env Finalized

1The big idea

FRD 0008.4 draws a hard line between the customer-owned Azure infrastructure that must already exist and the per-session sandbox that the runtime creates on demand.

In plain terms: there is exactly one customer-owned Sandbox Group per Function App / environment. The customer's own IaC provisions that group, its region, policies, identities, egress setup, state account, image reference, and scoped RBAC. At request time, the runtime creates only an individual sandbox inside that pre-provisioned group.

◆Customer plane

ARM/IaC creates the durable resources once per app/environment, in the customer's subscription and chosen region.

◆Runtime plane

The controller holds only the data-plane right to create, resume, and delete per-session sandboxes inside that one group.

◆Residency stays local

Because the group is customer-provisioned, subscription, region, quota, billing, and cleanup remain under the app owner's control.

2IaC plane vs runtime plane

The split is intentionally simple: powerful infrastructure rights never sit in the request-time runtime.

Provisioning boundary: durable infrastructure is IaC-owned; runtime actions stay inside the configured group. Session content still arrives via 0008.6; outbound auth remains mediated by 0008.9.

✗ Rejected: a central shared Sandbox Group

A Microsoft-owned multi-tenant group would centralize customer content, billing, quotas, and blast radius, and would require a new always-on operator plane. Decision 30 keeps the trust boundary in the customer's tenant instead.

4Residency, image, and identity

Three adjacent design areas all preserve the same boundary: customer-owned placement, runtime-published generic bits, and no identity inside the sandbox.

◆Data residency

The customer selects the Sandbox Group region in IaC. v1 is single-region per app/environment; multi-region and DR are deferred customer-IaC concerns. The state account should stay co-regional with the group.

◆Bootstrap image

Customer IaC references a runtime-published, digest-pinned stdlib-only bootstrap image. The customer controls where and which version runs; the image contains no MAF or app code. Packaging details live in 0008.6.

◆Identity-less sandbox

The sandbox has no storage access and no secrets. The controller delivers captured content; outbound authentication is mediated by the egress proxy, keeping tokens outside the sandbox (0008.9).

◆Authoring values

Retention uses a hierarchy: group default from ARM, app-level session_runtime.retention in v1, and a deferred per-agent override. The authoring surface is owned by 0008.10.

5Why this split matters

Decision 30 is not just deployment bookkeeping; it is the safety model for the whole feature.

◆Blast radius

A runaway app consumes only the customer's own group capacity and scoped quota, not a shared central pool used by other tenants.

◆Ownership

The party that creates durable resources also pays for them, chooses their region, grants roles, and removes them when the app/environment goes away.

◆Least privilege

The runtime can manage the sandboxes it creates, but cannot quietly expand infrastructure, alter residency, or broaden access beyond the one configured group.
