# FRD 0008.12 — Session/run lifecycle, failure behavior & reconciler/reaper

> Sub-FRD of [FRD 0008](0008-aca-sandbox-session-runtime.md). It re-expresses,
> in plain language, one self-contained decision area from the reviewed parent, and
> now also records the **cross-sibling coordination** that refined it (see the note
> below). It keeps the parent's decisions intact but splits their delivery across
> **v1** and **v2** where later sibling scoping required it. **Status: Finalized (2026-07-24)**
> after cross-sibling coordination (0008.2–0008.10) and an independent coherence review.

## What this is and why it matters

A session and its **ACA Sandbox** (an isolated Azure Container Apps container) move
through states over their lifetime — being created, running a turn, idling,
suspended, eventually deleted — and lots can go wrong along the way (storage
outages, lost create responses, crashes, controller recycles). This area defines
the **state machines**, the **table of required failure behaviors**, and the
**periodic reconciler/reaper** (a minimal timer plus opportunistic fast-paths) that keeps
abandoned sandboxes, snapshots, and records from leaking.

It also settles a load-bearing architectural question: **why v1 does *not* require
Durable Functions.** Durable Functions is Azure's framework for reliable multi-step
workflows (automatic retries, timers, queues, fan-out). It would be the obvious
tool for orchestrating this — but the design deliberately keeps a *small,
hand-written, no-retry* state machine instead, because the live process is owned by
the sandbox (0008.5) and the in-flight state is owned by the sandbox journal, so
the controller only needs simple conditional transitions plus one
create/resume/submit step.

> **Cross-sibling coordination & the v1/v2 line.** This area sits downstream of several
> siblings, whose finalized decisions reshaped what ships in v1 vs. v2:
> - **0008.8** deferred the owner-scoped external **Blob mirror** (its Decision 18) to
>   **v2**. So the *terminal-checkpoint mirror job* and *two-minute mirror SLO*
>   (Decision 24), the *mirror-before-reap* ordering, and *rebuild-from-checkpoint*
>   recovery are **v2**; v1 keeps a leaner reconciler.
> - **0008.3 / 0008.5** confirm the v1 durability split: the **Azure Tables** control
>   record is durable (terminal *status*, once recorded — by the controller, a request, or the periodic reconciler — survives a reaped sandbox); transcript/result
>   *content* lives only in the sandbox until reap.
> - **0008.6** scoped v1 to **Path 1 only**, so per-deploy *committed disk images*
>   (Path 2) and their cleanup are future work, not a v1 reaper concern.
> - **0008.2 / 0008.4** pin the ownership/quota keying and the teardown boundary.
> Every such point is cross-referenced inline; no sibling's body is edited here.

## Decisions this sub-FRD owns

- **Decision 4 — Durable Functions dependency.** **Not required** for v1;
  reconsider only for cross-session orchestration or managed-retry needs.
- **Decision 21 — Controller coordination (reaffirms #4).** Keep a **minimal
  no-retry Table state machine** because ADC owns the live process; revisit Durable
  before adding queues, retries, fan-out, or compensation.
- **Decision 22 — Expired-session cleanup.** Keep a **registered reconciler/reaper**
  (a plain Functions **timer trigger** — *not* a Durable eternal orchestration). **v1 runs a
  minimal periodic reconciler** as the **guaranteed backstop**, on a **configurable cadence
  (relaxed default ≈ 1 h; operator-tunable up to several hours)**, augmented by
  **opportunistic fast-paths** — a fire-and-forget sweep **after each create**, a synchronous
  **reap-on-capacity-failure → retry**, and the **controller inline fast-path** (frees a
  session's own slot on its next request) — plus **client polling** (any
  `get_status`/`get_result` reconciles that session inline). The fast-paths + polling handle
  the common case promptly; the low-frequency timer is the **irreducible floor** for what
  nothing else is *guaranteed* to catch: a **crashed** harness that never journals or polls (a
  request or create would catch it too, but none is guaranteed to arrive — only the periodic
    sweep always runs, detecting the **stale liveness signal**, then **confirming death before
    writing `abandoned`**), and re-arming
  auto-suspend on a terminal sandbox left in 0008.8 D27's run-disabled state (**idle-policy
  repair**). This **restores** the parent's original *"register a timer-triggered
  reconciler/reaper"* intent (master-log Decision 22, 2026-07-20), refined to *minimal +
  adaptive + fast-path-augmented*; an earlier interim "no v1 timer" framing was **withdrawn**
  once the crash-before-signal case proved a periodic sweep is mandatory. **Decision 4 stands**
  — a timer trigger is not Durable Functions.
- **Decision 24 — Reconciliation cadence.** Keep storage credentials out of the sandbox.
  **v1 runs the minimal periodic reconciler (Decision 22)** — a relaxed, configurable cadence
  (≈ 1 h default) for the backstop duties — but **no checkpoint-mirror reconciliation**. The
  **terminal-checkpoint mirror job** and its **two-minute p95** SLO are **v2** (deferred with
  the external Blob mirror, 0008.8 Decision 18); the cadence **tightens to ≈ once-a-minute in
  v2** *solely* to hold that SLO. **This is the *same* registered timer — one reconciler, not
  two:** v2 does not add a second timer; it tightens the existing reconciler's cadence and
  folds the mirror duty into the same periodic pass. So Decisions 22 and 24 describe **one
  timer at two maturities** — v1: backstop-only at ≈ 1 h; v2: backstop **+** mirror at ≈ 1 min.

### State machines, flow, and failure

**Session states:**
```text
Creating -> Ready -> Running -> Ready
                   -> Canceling -> Ready
Ready -> Suspending -> Suspended -> Resuming -> Ready
Any nonterminal state -> Failed
Ready/Suspended/Failed -> Deleting -> Deleted
```
**Run states** (canonical **lowercase** wire / stored `RunState` enum):
```text
accepted -> running -> succeeded | failed | timed_out
                    -> canceled          (`canceling` = transient internal step, NOT stored)
any nonterminal run whose sandbox is lost/superseded, OR whose harness dies
  silently (crash/OOM, no terminal written), -> abandoned
```
The **canonical stored/wire set is {`accepted`, `running`, `succeeded`, `failed`, `canceled`,
`timed_out`, `abandoned`}**; `canceling` is a transient controller phase during an explicit
cancel — **not** a persisted or surfaced `RunState` value.
Terminal semantics — **causes stated so no terminal is unclassified**: **`timed_out`** = the
backend watchdog fired on the **authored `timeout`** (0008.8); **`canceled`** = an **explicit
client cancel** *or* the **180 s sync-cap cancellation** (0008.11); **`failed`** = a
**harness-reported error** *or* a **controller-side output-validation failure** (0008.7's
invalid-output rule); **`abandoned`** = **no terminal was reported** (no-fault) —
infrastructure loss (sandbox **gone**) *or* silent harness death (crash/OOM before journaling),
and written **only after death is confirmed** (see *Single-writer safety*). Every terminal
frees the one-active-run slot (0008.2 Decision 6). `RunStatus.result` is set **only** for
**`succeeded`** while the result is retained; after eviction the status stays `succeeded`
with `result_available = false` and result reads return `410`. Wire spellings are settled
with 0008.11.

**Controller flow (happy path):** authenticate + resolve ownership → the **controller**
validates/generates the public `session_id`, generates `run_id`, and **assembles the
provisioning context** (owner hash, generation, captured script-root digest (Decision 68), Sandbox Group,
protocol version, egress, retention/lifecycle) from `OwnerContext` + app config + the session
record → conditionally create/read the session record → acquire the session lease → create
the sandbox if none exists (immutable disk + the **digest-addressed script-root content the
controller captures and delivers over the transport** (Decision 68 — the **SHA-256 over the
captured script-root zip**: code + vendored `.python_packages`; *not* an IaC-pinned
Run-From-Package deploy artifact) + labels + lifecycle + egress; record it only *after* protocol verification) →
resume + re-verify if suspended → **atomically admit** the run and submit the **minimal
per-turn `StartRunRequest` envelope** → for sync, wait for the terminal result and preserve
today's shape; for async, return `202` after the sandbox journals `accepted` → update the
directory, release the short lease, and re-arm auto-suspend after terminal. (`session_id`
minting and provisioning are **controller-side** — not carried in `StartRunRequest`.)

**The reconciler/reaper — and the crucial division of labor.** The **ACA platform
lifecycle policy** owns the routine idle→**suspend** transition (state-preserving, for
sub-second resume); the reconciler does **not** hand-roll idle-suspend. **Deletion**, by
contrast, is the **reaper's** job in v1: it owns the idle-expired sandbox + snapshot delete
so retention holds — a terminal async result must stay retrievable until fetched or the
reclaim window expires (0008.8). The customer's coarse **group-level `auto_delete`** is only
a far **backstop** (it deletes long-suspended sandboxes in a permanently-idle app); it is
*not* the authoritative deleter, and v1 does not depend on it for the precise window. Either
way, abandoned sandboxes/snapshots don't leak (Decision 22). What the platform fundamentally *cannot* do — and what the reconciler
uniquely exists for — is everything **outside** the sandbox:
- write terminal **`abandoned`** to the Tables record when a sandbox is lost, freeing the
  one-active-run slot — the reaper's one *irreducible* correctness duty (ACA deleting a
  container never touches your ledger);
- clean up the **external Table session/run records** (plus any snapshots/packages the
  runtime created that the platform won't auto-remove);
- repair stale controller state from sandbox journals.

Every reaper write/delete is idempotent, label-keyed (app/owner/session), and
generation-guarded; it never adopts a caller-supplied ID and never deletes a *newer*
generation. The reconciler runs on a **minimal periodic timer** (configurable, ≈ 1 h default)
**plus opportunistic fast-paths** — a sweep after each create, a reap-on-capacity-failure
retry, the controller fast-path, and any client poll (see *Why a minimal periodic
reconciler*). The **v2** addition is the terminal-checkpoint mirror job (Decision 24). It
**never** reaps standing customer infrastructure — see *V1 vs. v2 reconciler scope*. Admission
relies on **ACA's live
capacity** — v1 enforces no runtime quota counter — see *Active-session quotas*.

**Failure behavior** is specified as the **required table** below (the failure matrix this
sub-FRD owns):

| Condition | Required v1 behavior | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Storage (Tables) unavailable** | **Fail closed** — reject; never fall back to an unowned/unrecorded sandbox | availability yielded to the ownership invariant |
| **Generation / live-manifest mismatch** | **Not-found** semantics + security event; **quarantine** (stop using) the suspect sandbox — don't silently rebuild/reuse it | fail-closed refusal, not evidence-keeping |
| **Generation rollback** (recreate tries an older gen) | **`409`** + security event | tombstoned terminal rows make this observable |
| **Duplicate submit** (same owner/session/idempotency-key hash) | Resolve to the **existing `run_id`** — never a second process | idempotent admission |
| **Concurrent run** (a live run holds the slot) | Flat **`409`** with the active `run_id` | no supersede/queue in v1 |
| **Functions recycle after async ack** | Run **continues in the sandbox**; the next request **or periodic reconciler pass** adopts terminal from its non-rotating `status.json`/`result.json` + Tables record | controller is stateless across the handoff |
| **Sandbox genuinely lost mid-run** (deleted / disk unrecoverable) | Run **`abandoned`** + **session tombstoned** → the same `session_id` returns `410`; client creates a new session (rebuild-from-checkpoint = v2) | the session is dead, not migrated |
| **Generation superseded / rebind** (state-preserving) — **v2 only** | In-flight run **`abandoned`**, but the **session continues** on the new generation (resume from the last committed checkpoint); **not** tombstoned. **v1: this row does not occur** — a generation advance needs the external mirror, so in v1 a lost/superseded backing is **always** a tombstone/`410` | a bump is a *rebind*, not a loss; generation advance is **v2-only** (0008.3, 7c66c07) |
| **Redeploy** (captured script-root digest change) | Lazily, on the **next request to a stale-digest session**: **drain** (short grace to finish, else **`abandoned`**) → **tombstone** → `410`; client creates a new session on the current epoch; the orphaned old-epoch sandbox is **idle-reaped** within the retention window | v1: **no** durable continuity across a redeploy (v2); digest stamped by 0008.3 (row 70) |
| **Harness error (caught)** | Harness writes terminal **`failed`** (atomic); session stays recoverable when checkpoint integrity passes | agent/harness fault |
| **Harness silent death** (crash / OOM — no terminal written; **sandbox intact**) | Reconciler sees a **stale heartbeat** (a *suspicion*) → **verifies death** (probe → verified process-group kill, or sandbox-reclaim if still unverifiable) → *then* run **`abandoned`** + free slot + re-arm idle policy (**sandbox retained** on confirmed-death-with-intact-disk); **session continues** — resubmit resumes on the same sandbox from the last committed checkpoint (0008.8) | never free the slot while a process may hold the OS lock; only the periodic sweep is *guaranteed* to catch it |
| **Terminal vs. cancel race** | Terminal **success/failure wins** the cancellation race | monotonic transitions |
| **Forced-cancel timeout** | Escalate to a **verified process-group kill**; if the kill still can't confirm death, **escalate to sandbox-reclaim** (destroying the sandbox produces *definitive* death) — only *then* write the terminal (`canceled` if killed, `abandoned` if reclaimed); **never** free `active_run_id` by status-overwrite while a process may hold the OS lock | the OS-lock is the final concurrency backstop |
| **Result fetched after reap** | `get_result` → **`result-evicted` / `410`**; status still known from Tables | v1 durability split |

**Recovery of a lost sandbox is version-scoped:** in **v2**, rebuild from
the latest *verified* external checkpoint; in **v1** (no external mirror) terminal *status*
still survives in the Tables record but *content* does not, so a gone sandbox is **`410
Gone`** for the session. See *Losing a sandbox mid-run* below. **Generation model (0008.3,
95f5caf):** v1 **loss ⇒ tombstone / `410`, *never* a generation bump**. A generation *bump* is a
**state-preserving rebind to a *different* sandbox instance**; the **v2** rebuild-from-checkpoint
is exactly that rebind (a bump), **not** a fresh post-loss generation — and it is possible only
while the **external mirror survives**; if the mirror is *also* gone (**true loss**) even v2
tombstones. (v2's exact rebind semantics are deferred.) **Suspend/resume and a
crash-with-intact-sandbox keep the *same* generation** (no rebind). A generation **advance is
v2-only** — it needs the external mirror to reconstruct state on a different backing (0008.3,
7c66c07); **v1 has no state-preserving rebind, so a lost/superseded backing is *always* a
tombstone**.

### V1 vs. v2 reconciler scope (what it reaps, what it never touches)

The reconciler reclaims **per-sandbox / per-session** resources only. The ACA lifecycle
policy already **suspends** idle compute (→ cheap); **deletion is the reaper's** in v1 (the
coarse group-level `auto_delete` is only a far backstop). Its surface: the **Table
session/run records** (**tombstoned** on reclaim, via ETag-CAS — see below), the
idle-expired **sandbox + snapshot** (ADC data-plane delete), and any content-packages the
runtime created that the platform won't auto-remove — always idempotently, label-keyed,
generation-guarded.

**On reclaim, terminal rows are tombstoned, not hard-deleted (Decision 22).** A tombstone retains the
row's **monotonic generation** after the session ends, so a same-`session_id` recreate
cannot roll the generation backward — closing the delete-then-recreate edge. That edge is
already outside 0008.3's threat model (opaque, non-reused `session_id`s + controller-MI
as the sole row writer), so this is cheap **defense-in-depth**; the trade-off is a tiny
retained-row cost vs. the rollback-ghost protection. (Endorsed by 0008.3.)

It **never** reaps standing, customer-owned infrastructure. These are **customer-owned
teardown**, symmetric with creation (0008.4 Decision 30): the **Sandbox Group**, the
base disk image, the disk-image reference, the dedicated state account, managed
identities, the egress policy, and RBAC. Stating this keeps the
runtime's data-plane rights deliberately narrow.

*Per-deploy committed disk images* (0008.6 Path 2) are **out of scope and deferred** —
v1 ships **Path 1 only**, so no committed images exist yet. When Path 2 ships, their GC
stays **deploy/customer-side** (least-privilege: the runtime must not hold image
delete/manage rights). The reaper would contribute only *indirectly* — normal
per-session reclamation **drains references** so a superseded image becomes eligible,
and the deploy-side owner then deletes it honoring the platform's *child-before-parent*
order. Moving image GC into the runtime would be an explicit Decision 30 rights change.

### Single-writer safety (Q2 → resolved & signed off)

Two writers could touch a session/run record at once — two concurrent opportunistic
reconciles (e.g. two creates sweeping at the same time), or a reconcile racing the
controller's own inline repair. v1 guarantees single-writer
*correctness* **without** a distributed leader-election lock (which would drift back
toward the orchestration complexity Decisions 4/21 avoid), in three layers:

1. **No correctness-critical lock.** v1 has a periodic reconciler *and* opportunistic fast-paths
   (reap-after-create / reap-on-capacity-failure / fast-path / client poll), so several
   controller invocations — plus the periodic sweep — may reconcile the same session
   concurrently. That is safe purely because of layer 2, with **no leader-election lock**. The
   platform timer's built-in **singleton lease** serializes the *periodic* pass to one
   instance; the fast-paths need no such lease because layer 2 makes concurrent writers
   converge.
2. **Record level.** Every mutation is an **idempotent, ETag-conditional
   compare-and-swap** (Azure Tables, Decision 20) with **monotonic** transitions
   (nonterminal→terminal only; generation only increases). Concurrent writers converge:
   the loser re-reads, sees the terminal/newer state, and no-ops.
3. **Liveness authority — read, never lock.** The reconciler decides "is this run live?"
   from the **authoritative sandbox OS-lock/journal + generation** (0008.2, 0008.5) —
   *never* from the controller's short create/resume/submit lease, which is released after
   submission and is not a liveness signal. Destructive actions are gated on **generation
   guards** plus the ETag-CAS above: if the controller is concurrently operating on the
   session, the reconcile simply loses the compare-and-swap and no-ops — no lock needed.
   **Signed off (v1):** pure ETag-CAS + generation-guard is the committed mechanism; a
   *soft-yield* on an observably-held controller lease is an **optional** optimization to
   trim wasted contention, not required for correctness.

This also underwrites the **sole-deleter** assumption: the runtime (the reaper) is the
authoritative deleter of session sandboxes — ACA's idle-suspend never deletes, and the
coarse group `auto_delete` is only a backstop that can touch a *suspended* sandbox, never a
live/held one.

**Considered and rejected — deriving liveness from the platform.** A tempting
simplification is to drop the external "active" record and instead *compute* liveness at
submit time from live sandbox state, so a platform auto-delete would clear the "active"
condition by itself and the reaper's `abandoned` write would disappear. It is rejected
for two independent reasons. **(1) Atomicity.** Two simultaneous submits would both
observe "no active run" and both start — violating Decision 6. Only the **ETag-conditional
Tables write** gives a *linearizable* admission lock (read run-state + conditionally
create the non-terminal row in one compare-and-swap). A derived check is TOCTOU-racy.
**Admission is precisely a single-entity ETag CAS on the session row's `active_run_id`
pointer** (null → `run_id`), in the *same owner partition* as the run rows: an **entity-group
transaction** creates the non-terminal run row + the idempotency record *together with* the
pointer update. If `active_run_id` is already non-null the submit fails **`409
active_run_exists`** (after any stale-run reconciliation); the pointer clears **only on
terminal adoption under ETag**. A conditional row-*create* by itself is **not** the
one-active-run guard — the pointer CAS is.
**(2) A platform read cannot prove a live process stopped** (0008.2's two-lock rationale);
a stale "not running" observation that let a *second* run start is a worse failure than a
stale row that merely wedges at `409`. The design therefore keeps the external ETag gate
for admission and uses the sandbox journal only as the **liveness authority** for
reconciling a stale gate — which is exactly the model above, and precisely why the
reaper's `abandoned` write is irreducible (the stale external flag must be actively
cleared). A *hybrid* that kept platform-derived liveness while preserving ETag atomicity
collapses into this same committed model.

### Losing a sandbox mid-run: the abandon path & the v1 durability split (open Q5 → resolved)

When a sandbox is **genuinely lost** (gone / disk unrecoverable) while a run is in flight, the
in-flight run is **abandoned, not migrated** (0008.5): in-flight state was never
mirrored, and the lost sandbox can no longer journal its own death. (A **generation
supersede/rebind** (**v2-only**) is *different* — a **state-preserving** rebind that abandons
the in-flight *run* but keeps the **session** alive on the new generation; it is **not** a
loss/tombstone. **In v1 there is no such rebind** (a generation advance needs the external
mirror), so a lost/superseded backing is **always** a tombstone; 0008.3 owns the exact
generation semantics (7c66c07).) Because 0008.2's
one-active-run slot frees **only** on a terminal state, **the reconciler is the only
actor that can supply that terminal** — otherwise the session would wedge at `409`
forever. This is a v1 duty in the Decision 22/24 area.

The reconciler classifies each **non-terminal** run by inspecting its sandbox (via the ADC
data plane, from outside — no sandbox egress), in this order:
- **Adopt-terminal (v1) → durable mirror (v2).** Sandbox **reachable** and its non-rotating
  `status.json`/`result.json` holds a terminal → **adopt `succeeded`/`failed`/`timed_out`/`canceled` into the Tables
  record** (inline ETag-CAS — no Blob; the sandbox is right there). **v2** additionally
  **mirrors the terminal *content*** to the external checkpoint so it survives sandbox *loss*
  (rebuild-from-checkpoint). v1 fixes *status*; v2 adds *content* durability.
- **Crash detection → abandon the run, RETAIN the sandbox; session CONTINUES (v1).** Sandbox
  and disk **intact** but the run crashed — **confirmed process death** (the harness's
  OS-lock / process-group is observably released, *or* a **verified process-group kill**
  succeeded — coordinating with 0008.2's verified-kill + `active_run_id` CAS) with **no terminal
  written** → write terminal **`abandoned`**, clear `active_run_id`, and **free the
  one-active-run slot**. **A stale heartbeat alone is a *suspicion*, not proof:** the **harness
  emits a heartbeat every ~30 s** (an *emission* interval, **not** a reconciler probe cadence —
  the v1 reconciler is the ~1 h backstop), and a heartbeat is considered **stale after ~3 missed
  emissions (~90 s)** (values are impl-readiness-tunable defaults). When the reconciler (on its
  backstop cadence or a fast-path) observes a stale heartbeat it only **triggers a verification
  probe** — solely a *confirmed* lock/process-group death **or** a **verified process-group
  kill** authorizes the `abandoned` write and slot release. **If death stays unverifiable**, the
  reconciler must **escalate to sandbox-reclaim** (destroying the sandbox produces *definitive*
  death) *before* writing `abandoned`; it **never** frees `active_run_id` by status-overwrite
  while a process may still hold the OS lock. A merely-slow harness still holding its lock is
  thus **never** falsely abandoned. **Crucially,
  run-abandon ≠ sandbox-reclaim:** freeing the slot on a recoverable crash **never deletes the
  sandbox** — the reconciler only re-arms idle policy. **The session survives:** the sandbox
  (with its last **committed checkpoint**, 0008.8) is retained, so a **resubmit starts a fresh
  run on the *same* sandbox, resuming from that checkpoint** (the controller's
  self-heal-stale-slot fast-path applies here). **Sandbox reclaim (deletion) fires only on**
  (i) genuine loss (**gone / disk unrecoverable** → tombstone → `410`, below), (ii) **idle-retention
  expiry** — nobody resumed within the window, or (iii) a **forced reclaim to produce definitive
  death** when a crashed run's death is otherwise unverifiable (above). A legitimately *quiet* run (e.g. a long model
  call) still emits heartbeats / holds its lock, so it is **not** falsely abandoned.
- **Lost sandbox → abandon the run + TOMBSTONE the session (v1).** Sandbox/snapshot
  **genuinely gone** (disk unrecoverable) → write terminal **`abandoned`**, free the slot, **and
  tombstone the session row** → the same `session_id` returns **`410`** (the session is dead;
  the client must **create a new session**). The reaper does **not** mint a new generation on
  the dead `session_id` — v1 loss is **never** a bump. Rebuild-from-checkpoint is a **v2**
  capability and is a **state-preserving rebind (= generation bump), not a fresh generation**,
  possible only while the **external mirror survives**; if the mirror is also gone (**true
  loss**) even v2 tombstones (exact v2 semantics deferred).

**Adopt-before-abandon ordering** stops a completed run being mislabeled: a reachable sandbox
showing a verified terminal is adopted *before* any abandon decision. All of this runs on the
**periodic reconciler** (guaranteed within its cadence) *and* on the opportunistic fast-paths
+ any client poll (promptly). So a detached async run that succeeds after a controller recycle
is read back correctly — and a **crashed** run (which can never self-report) is caught by the
sweep. The reconciler is the **only** actor that catches a crash: precisely why a periodic
sweep is irreducible (see *Why a minimal periodic reconciler*). (The v2 durable-content mirror
is the *mirror-before-reap* invariant 0008.5's cross-instance reattach recovery depends on.)

**Detection is multi-path**, all writing the same idempotent terminal:
1. **Controller inline fast-path.** On the next request, if the controller finds a
   non-terminal run whose sandbox is gone or crashed, it reconciles to terminal *before* the
   one-active-run check — so the very resubmit that would have hit a phantom `409` instead
   clears the stale slot and proceeds. (Handled **inline**, not deferred.)
2. **Opportunistic sweeps.** The after-create sweep and the reap-on-capacity-failure path
   catch stragglers for sessions being actively created against / contended.
3. **Periodic reconciler (the guaranteed floor).** On its configurable cadence (≈ 1 h
   default) it catches what 1–2 cannot: a **crashed** run and a **zero-poll** run in an
   otherwise-idle app — the cases with no request and no create to ride on.

This upgrades 0008.2's guarantee to *"freed on the next submit/poll, on the next create /
capacity-failure sweep, or on the periodic reconciler's next pass — whichever is first."*

**The v1 durability split** (why the above is necessary):
- The sandbox event journal `events.jsonl` **rotates** (last 16×1 MiB; count-based) and
  bounds streaming *replay depth* only — **not** durability. Terminal state lives in the
  separate, **non-rotating** `status.json`/`result.json`, per run under
  `runs/{run_id}/` (0008.5). Terminal-state reconstruction therefore reads status/result,
  **never** by replaying the journal.
- Terminal **status** (`succeeded`/`failed`/`timed_out`/`canceled`/`abandoned`) is durable in the **Azure Tables**
  control record (0008.3 Decision 20) → survives a reaped sandbox and answers `get_status`.
  Every run reaches a terminal record **within the reconciler's cadence at the latest**: the
  controller records it (sync), a request adopts it (polled), or the periodic reconciler
  adopts the true terminal (reachable sandbox) or writes `abandoned` (crashed/lost). There is
  no "never-observed" hole — the sweep observes every non-terminal run regardless of client
  activity. The terminal **row** is retained for the **retention window**, then
  **pruned/archived** (after which `get_status` also returns `410`) — which keeps the reaper's
  direct scan **bounded** to the live + recently-terminal working set rather than growing with
  all history.
- Terminal **result content** (`result.json`) lives only in the live sandbox in v1. It is
  protected from reaping for the **full idle-retention window measured from the terminal
  transition** — auto-suspend is disabled during a run (0008.8 Decision 27), so the idle
  clock starts at terminal and the full window is available. **Auto-suspend is safe** (it
  preserves `result.json`); only *destroy/reap* loses it, so the reaper gates the destroy,
  not the suspend. The result stays fetchable **until fetched or the idle-retention window
  expires, whichever comes first**; retrieving it **releases the hold** and the session
  reverts to normal idle-retention (kept for reuse); immediate reap after fetch is an
  explicit one-shot opt-in only. After the window, `get_result` returns **`result-evicted`**
  (HTTP `410`) while status remains known from Tables.
- **Restoring auto-suspend at terminal (idle-policy repair).** Because D27 *disables*
  auto-suspend during a run, it must be **re-enabled** at terminal or the sandbox stays awake
  (billed). The re-arm is always **controller-side** — the sandbox is sealed and
  credential-less, so it never rewrites its own lifecycle policy (that would need
  management-plane creds; rejected across 0008.5/0008.6/0008.8). Who re-arms it:
  - **sync run:** the controller does it inline (present at terminal);
  - **detached async / polled:** the **next `get_status`/`get_result`** reconciles inline
    (reads `status.json`, adopts terminal, re-arms) — the harness's `status.json` write *is* the
    creds-free "done" signal;
  - **crashed / zero-poll / idle-app:** the **periodic reconciler** re-arms it on its cadence
    — the guaranteed floor, since a crashed or never-polled run has no other actor. The awake
    tail before re-arm is bounded by the **configured** cadence — ≈ 1 h at the default, but as
    much as several hours if an operator relaxes it (the warmth-vs-spend knob cuts both ways),
    assuming a healthy timer. Any poll re-arms sooner; v2 auto-tightens to ~1 min.

  The reconciler reads `status.json` **from outside via the ACA data plane** (the controller's
  existing channel — *no* sandbox egress, *no* sandbox lifecycle rights). *Optional promptness
  layer (not v1):* an in-sandbox data-plane self-suspend-*request* could trim the tail **iff**
  a spike confirms the verb on `0.1.0b3` + 0008.6 grants one narrow egress; a
  sandbox→controller callback is rejected — it breaks 0008.5's pull-only transport, can't catch
  a crash (so the periodic reconciler is needed regardless), and is thus **dominated**: since
  restore is a free rider on the mandatory reconciler, a callback would be *additive* (callback
  + timer), and it still cold-starts a scaled-to-zero app to receive. *Optional D27-owned hardening:* a long finite idle timeout (≤ 1 h,
  the ACA max) instead of full-disable caps the awake tail even if the reconciler is slow —
  but only where runs never go quiet > 1 h (else a live run naps mid-flight, D27's own
  rationale); full-disable + reconciler stays the robust default.
- **v2** upgrades this: the external mirror makes result content survive reaping, so the
  retention window degrades from a durability guard to a warm-resume convenience.

### Redeploy: drain-on-deploy (script-root-digest epochs)

A **redeploy** is a change to the app's **captured script-root digest** — the **SHA-256 over
the controller-captured script-root zip** (code + vendored `.python_packages`), Decision 68,
**stamped on the session by 0008.3** at create (the *captured* content, **not** an IaC-pinned
Run-From-Package deploy artifact). A redeploy cuts a new **epoch**; a session belongs to the epoch it was
created in. A **worker restart or scale-out reusing the *same* digest is *not* a redeploy** and
never drains — the session simply continues. Detection is **lazy**: on the **next request to a
session whose captured script-root digest ≠ the controller's current digest**, the controller
**drains** it — the in-flight run gets a **short grace to finish**, otherwise it is written
**`abandoned`** (the existing terminal, freeing the slot) — then **tombstones the session** so
the same `session_id` returns **`410`** and the client **creates a new session** on the current
epoch. Old-epoch sandboxes orphaned by the cut are **idle-reaped by the reconciler within the
idle-retention window** — the same reap path, which also **bounds the "indefinitely-old
sandbox" concern** (no stale-epoch sandbox outlives its retention). **v1 scope:** there is **no
durable continuity across a redeploy** — carrying a live session onto the new epoch is a **v2**
capability (needs the external checkpoint mirror). (Master-log row 70.)

### Session retention policy (idle durations)

Retention is **idle-based** — 0008.8 owns auto-suspend behavior but *defers the retention
policy here* (0008.8's *Honest critique*). Two durations govern it, and v1 uses a **hybrid
(group default + runtime per-sandbox override)**:

| Duration | What it does | v1 default | Range / source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Auto-suspend idle timeout** | idle → freeze (memory-preserving nap; warm resume) | **~5 min (300 s)** | ACA supports 60 / 120 / 300 / 600 / 1800 / 3600 s (0008.8) |
| **Idle-retention (reclaim) window** | idle → reclaim the session (delete sandbox/snapshot; **tombstone** the record) | **~24 h idle** | configurable; product/cost knob |

**How the values are set — the two-plane split, and two enforcement paths.** ACA exposes
lifecycle at *two* levels (verified against ACA Sandboxes docs):
- The **Sandbox-Group lifecycle policy** (ARM control plane) is the customer-owned
  **default/fallback** — created in IaC alongside the group (0008.4 Decision 30). We never
  reconfigure it.
- **Per-sandbox lifecycle** is settable on the **ADC data plane** (the plane the runtime
  already uses — 0008.5): `set_lifecycle_policy(...)` / `aca sandbox lifecycle set --id`.

The two durations are enforced by **different, both-proven** mechanisms — so a **global /
app-level config resets both** *without the customer editing the group IaC*:
- **Auto-suspend timeout → ACA per-sandbox lifecycle-set.** The runtime writes the
  auto-suspend value per-sandbox at create (`create → set_lifecycle_policy → ensure_ready
  → submit`; one extra data-plane op, no transport-verb change — 0008.5). **Proven on SDK
  `0.1.0b3`** (0008.5's spike exercised auto-suspend control; 0008.8 Decision 27 already
  depends on per-sandbox lifecycle writes, so this is no new privilege). The override value
  is **persisted** (app/global config, denormalized onto the session control record at
  create) so 0008.8's D27 **restore targets the override, not the group default** —
  otherwise the first run would silently downgrade the session to the group fallback.
- **Reclaim window → reaper-enforced delete.** The reaper is the **sole authoritative
  deleter**, and — via the **periodic reconciler + fast-paths** — it runs **regardless of app
  activity**, so there is no "idle app has no actor" gap. Once idle exceeds the reclaim window
  it deletes the session's **sandbox + snapshot** via the **ADC data-plane delete** (a proven
  op) and **tombstones** the Table record via ETag-CAS. **Adopt-before-reap:** before deleting
  a sandbox whose Tables row is still non-terminal, the reaper reads `status.json` and
  **adopts** any terminal (`succeeded`/`failed`/`timed_out`/`canceled`; else, only after confirming
  stale-liveness or a gone sandbox, `abandoned`), so terminal **status** is
  captured no later than reclaim. This satisfies 0008.8's two contracts by construction:
  **(C1) idle-through-suspend + reset-on-request** — the reclaim clock is idle wall-clock from
  terminal, reset by any request, ticking *during* the suspend nap (the two precisions above),
  never absolute-from-create; **(C2) a single authoritative deleter** — the reaper
  (generation-guarded, 0008.3 single-writer). **No deleter races a *live* session:** a live
  run has auto-suspend disabled (0008.8 D27), and any `auto_delete` only ever deletes
  *suspended* sandboxes — so neither the platform nor the reaper can touch a running session.

  The customer's group-level `auto_delete` (never an absolute-from-create TTL) is only a
  **coarse far-backstop** — it reclaims an **already-suspended** sandbox if the reconciler is
  down long enough to miss it. It is **not** on the v1 reclaim path; the reaper is.
  **Backstop-ordering invariant (0008.12 owns; 0008.10 enforces at config/registration):** the
  authored `reclaim_idle` must satisfy **`reclaim_idle ≤ auto_delete − cadence − grace`**
  (equivalently `auto_delete ≥ reclaim_idle + cadence + grace`) so the group backstop can never
  pre-empt the reaper and silently truncate the authored retention. **Terms** (all **seconds**):
  `auto_delete` = the group idle→delete backstop; `cadence` = the **effective configured reaper
  interval** (v1 ~1 h default — the same reaper knob, **not** the ~1-min v2 mirror cadence;
  validate against the ceiling if operators may raise it); `grace` = a **fixed 0008.12-owned
  safety buffer** (clock skew + adoption/execution time + partial-tick), **default ~5 min
  (300 s)**. The comparison is **inclusive `≤`** (grace already supplies the margin), so 0008.10
  **fails closed** on strictly-greater **when the group `auto_delete` is readable** at config
  time (remedy: lower `reclaim_idle` or raise the group `auto_delete` in IaC); **when the group
  backstop is *not* readable** at config time it **warns rather than blocks**, because the reaper
  is the runtime floor — at reclaim time it reads the group policy and reclaims at
  **`min(reclaim_idle, auto_delete − cadence − grace)`**, so a too-large authored value is
  **clamped** (a clean early reclaim, logged) rather than a surprise group force-delete. v1
  *core correctness* (no live-session deletion, single authoritative deleter) does **not** depend
  on this at all — the reconciler reclaims first by design; the invariant only protects the
  authored-retention *promise*. **Degraded-mode caveat:** because the backstop only ever
  deletes *suspended* sandboxes, it can reclaim a suspended session during a reconciler
  outage — but a sandbox left **awake** by an outage that struck *before* idle-policy repair
  stays awake (and thus un-backstoppable) until the reconciler recovers. Accordingly the
  "awake tail ≤ one cadence" guarantee **assumes a healthy timer**; a prolonged timer outage
  degrades it. Letting ACA's per-sandbox `auto_delete` become the
  *happy-path* deleter is a **future optimization** (0008.5 flags its `0.1.0b3` surface
  unverified → a targeted spike, not a new gate), viable only if it honors C1 natively; then
  the split would flip to *platform = happy-path, reaper = backstop/reconciler*. In v1 it is
  *reaper = authoritative; group `auto_delete` = coarse far-backstop only*.

The v1 override is **app-scoped, not per-agent**: it is authored in the **app-level
`session_runtime.retention` block** (`agents.config.yaml`, owned + validated by 0008.10) —
*not* in per-agent `.agent.md` front matter — so 0008.10's deployment-vs-authoring rule
holds (retention is never per-*agent* in v1). The override is **author-owned** (declared
in-repo by the app developer, version-controlled) — chosen 2026-07-23 over an
operator-owned deploy-time alternative for simplicity and because it is already shipped;
an **operator-owned override layer** (precedence *built-in defaults < author config <
operator deploy-setting*) is recorded as a clean **deferred future** enhancement, not v1.
0008.10 owns the field + validation
(`auto_suspend_idle` ∈ {60/120/300/600/1800/3600} s; `reclaim_idle` positive and >
`auto_suspend_idle`; rejected unless the backend is `aca_sandbox`); **0008.12 owns the
defaults + the per-sandbox-at-create application.** The per-sandbox write + reaper delete
sit within the runtime's data-plane scope (0008.4 Decision 30, confirmed). *Load note:* the
per-create lifecycle write is +1 data-plane op per create (create-path, not per-turn),
folded into 0008.5's 100-concurrent load-gate budget.

Both clocks are **idle-based and start at the terminal transition** (auto-suspend is
disabled during a run — 0008.8 Decision 27 — so a finished result gets the *full*
retention window from terminal). Two precisions (agreed with 0008.8, stated identically):

- **The reclaim clock runs *through* the suspend nap** — it is idle **wall-clock from
  terminal**, not gated on the sandbox being awake. So a suspended-and-abandoned session
  still ages toward reclaim; otherwise it would nap forever and `410` would be
  meaningless. Abandoned-session lifecycle: *terminal → ~5 min → **suspend** (cheap,
  warm-resumable) → … → ~24 h idle from terminal → **reclaim/delete → 410***.
- **Any request resets the idle clock** — including `get_status`/`get_result` (each is a
  request to the session, served via warm resume). So an **actively-polling client keeps
  the session alive** (the result stays fetchable), while an **abandoned one reaps** after
  ~24 h idle. This mirrors ACA's own "any request resets the cooldown" model. The only
  bound on a session kept alive by perpetual polling is the *deferred* absolute
  max-lifetime ceiling (below).

The **reclaim window is also the result-retention window**: a terminal async result stays
fetchable until the session is reclaimed (~24 h idle with no requests), then `get_result`
→ `410`. There is **no absolute creation-time TTL** in v1
(an actively-used session is never killed on a clock). The defaults above are starting
points, **not hard-coded** — set from the **app-level `session_runtime.retention`**
override (0008.10) when present, else 0008.12's defaults, applied per-sandbox by the
runtime (group policy is the fallback); precedence **app-level override > group default**.
**Per-*agent* retention** (a per-`.agent.md` authored override) is **out of v1** — reserved
as a future (v2) enhancement, at which point precedence extends to *per-agent > app-level >
group*. An optional absolute max-lifetime ceiling could likewise be layered later for
cost/compliance (deferred).

### Active-session quotas (open Q4 → v1: no caps; per-owner deferred to v2)

Nothing may allow *unbounded creates* — but in **v1 the runtime enforces no quota
counter of its own**. Admission relies on the platform:

- **Aggregate limit → ACA's live Sandbox-Group capacity.** A create beyond the group's
  capacity is rejected by ACA. Capacity is freed by the **reaper** deleting idle-expired
  sandboxes; crucially, on an ACA "no capacity" rejection the controller does a
  **synchronous reap-then-retry** (reconcile known-dead sessions to terminal, delete their
  sandboxes, retry once) — so an expired-but-not-yet-swept slot is reclaimed **exactly when
  capacity is contended**, without a standing counter or admission table read.
  (0008.4/0008.5 already validate the 100-concurrent target against ACA's own capacity.)
- **No per-owner cap in v1.** A fixed per-owner ceiling would cap a *single-owner* app's
  one owner well below the app's real capacity (wasting it), so v1 **omits per-owner
  quota entirely**. A single owner can use the app's full ACA capacity.

**Deferred to v2 — per-owner fairness quota.** 0008.2 confirms multi-user-per-app is
supported (many distinct `entra_user` owners in one app), so a *fairness* ceiling — *one
user must not starve others in a shared app* — is a real future need. Because ACA has no
concept of an *owner*, only the runtime can enforce it, by counting an owner's
non-terminal rows in the Table (keyed by owner-hash; `function_app` collapses to per-app).
It is deferred as a **v2 optimization**, opt-in per deployment. **Consequence to accept
for v1:** in a multi-user app, one user *could* consume the app's whole capacity; there is
no v1 fairness guard. (Flagged to 0008.2.)

> **Note — the Table is not a quota structure.** Even with no v1 quota, the authoritative
> Table row remains load-bearing for **durable ownership/authz**, the **one-active-run
> ETag admission lock** (Q2), **terminal-status durability** for `get_status` after reap,
> and **monotonic-generation rollback protection** (0008.3). Quota was only ever a cheap read
> on top; removing it removes no other function, and the Table stays.

### Why a minimal periodic reconciler — and still no Durable Functions

v1 runs a **minimal periodic reconciler** (a plain Functions timer trigger) as the guaranteed
backstop, on a **configurable, relaxed cadence — default ≈ 1 h** — augmented by opportunistic
fast-paths + client polling. An earlier draft tried to avoid the timer entirely
("opportunistic only"); the restore-mechanism investigation (with 0008.5 / 0008.6 / 0008.8 +
a research spike) showed that **doesn't close**: a **crashed** harness never journals a
terminal and can never self-signal (no callback, no self-suspend), so **only the periodic
controller-side sweep is *guaranteed* to detect it** and free its slot (a request or create
would too, but none may ever arrive). The timer is therefore the
**irreducible correctness floor**, not optional tidying.

**What the timer alone must guarantee** (the cases with no request/create to ride on):
- **Crash detection** — a run whose sandbox is reachable but whose harness process is
  **confirmed dead** (OS-lock/process-group released or a verified-kill; a stale heartbeat alone
  only triggers the verifying probe) with no terminal → `abandoned` + free the slot + re-arm idle policy (reclaim then follows the normal idle window).
- **Idle-policy repair** — re-arm auto-suspend on a terminal sandbox left in D27's
  run-disabled state, so ACA naps it → the compute stops being billed.
- **Reclaim** — delete a sandbox/snapshot past the idle-retention window; tombstone its row.

**Why the cadence can be relaxed (≈ 1 h, tunable).** The common case never waits for the
timer: **client polling** re-arms/adopts a monitored run inline on its next
`get_status`/`get_result`, the **after-create sweep** tidies active apps, and
**reap-on-capacity-failure** frees slots exactly when capacity is contended. The timer only
bites for **unmonitored, crashed, or fully-idle-app** sessions — a minority — and none of its
duties is latency-critical: reclaim runs on a ~24 h scale; crash→`abandoned` for a session
nobody polls blocks nobody (any returning client is unblocked inline by the fast-path); the
only cadence-sensitive cost is the **awake-compute tail** of an un-restored sandbox, bounded to
*one cadence* (assuming a healthy timer). Each tick finds due work with a **bounded query/scan directly over the single
source-of-truth session/run table** (0008.3's store) — there is **no separate due-work index in
v1**. The reaper scans **both row types**, because the due-time lives on different rows (0008.3
defines the exact placement): the **run-timeout deadline is on the RUN row**; **idle-reclaim
`expires_at` and `idle_policy_armed` are on the SESSION row**. Azure Tables indexes only
`PartitionKey`/`RowKey`, so the predicates are a scan:
- **run-timeout backstop** — a RUN row that is **`accepted` *or* `running`** (both must be
  covered — a crash *after acceptance but before running* must not stay unclassified) with
  **`expires_at <= now`** → timeout-abandon;
- **idle reclaim** — a SESSION row whose sandbox is terminal-idle with **`expires_at <= now`**
  → reclaim, but **only after an ETag-CAS confirms no active run exists** (`active_run_id =
  null`) so a resumed session is never deleted out from under a live run;
- **idle-policy repair** — a SESSION row that is **terminal `AND idle_policy_armed = false`**
  → arm the idle policy.

At v1 scale (~100 sessions) the scan is trivially cheap, and the mutation needs the
authoritative row anyway (re-read by `PartitionKey`/`RowKey` + ETag before writing). **The scan
set stays bounded** because terminal and tombstoned rows are **pruned/archived after the
retention window** (below) — the reaper only scans the *live + recently-terminal* working set,
not all history, so dropping the index never lets the scan grow unbounded. These due-time fields are the **canonical
0008.3 row contract** (0008.3 *persists* them; 0008.12 owns their *values/semantics*):
**`expires_at`** is the row's single next-due timestamp — state-dependent (a run row's
timeout-backstop deadline, a session row's reclaim deadline), and it **replaces** the
earlier `reclaim_at` name; **`idle_policy_armed`** (bool) distinguishes terminal-not-yet-armed
from terminal-armed-idle; **`last_activity_at`** is the last-activity timestamp that feeds
`expires_at` for idle rows. Deriving due work straight from the
authoritative table also **removes a consistency bug** a lagging separate index would
introduce: a run that crashes *between* its authoritative-row write and a separate index insert
can never be lost, because there is only **one** write. An idle app's tick finds nothing due —
near-free — and the cadence is **adaptive** (fast while runs are active, backing off when
nothing is pending). **v1 cadence is the ~1 h backstop** (operator-tunable, up to several
hours); the **~1-min cadence is the *v2* mirror-SLO target, *not* v1** — do not read v1 as
~1 min. In **v2** the single timer tightens to ≈ 1/min for the checkpoint-mirror SLO. *If* scan cost ever
bites (well beyond v1 scale), the **v2-only** optimization is a **same-partition secondary row
co-written atomically** with the authoritative row (one entity-group transaction, no lag) —
**not** a separate lagging table.

**Still no Durable Functions (Decision 4 stands).** A registered **timer trigger** is a plain
Functions primitive — *not* Durable Functions (no orchestrator, no replay, no durable
timers/queues/fan-out). Reinstating this minimal timer walks back only an over-aggressive
"no v1 timer" sub-refinement; it does **not** reintroduce the orchestration dependency
Decisions 4/21 avoid. Concurrent sweeps stay safe by the same ETag-CAS + generation-guard as
every other writer (see *Single-writer safety*); the platform timer's built-in **singleton
lease** serializes the periodic pass if desired.

**What cleanup actually touches (v1)** is deliberately small: the external **Table records**
(tombstoned), the **sandbox + snapshot** delete once retention is satisfied, and any
content-packages the runtime created — so abandoned sandboxes/snapshots don't leak
(Decision 22). It does *not* hand-roll idle→suspend; the ACA lifecycle policy does that. The
durable state it reconciles against is just the authoritative **Table row** (owner binding +
monotonic generation + status), read on every request for routing/ownership. (v1 keeps no
quota counter — per-owner quota is a v2 optimization.) There is **no signed-binding artifact
and no immutable log** in v1 (0008.3 Decisions 32-R/39-R); the monotonic generation is
**rollback protection**, not an evidence trail — so cleanup has nothing to preserve when it
deletes.

### No automatic retry & the resubmit pattern (open Q3 → resolved)

v1 has **no automatic run retry**: a failed run becomes terminal `failed`, and the
caller resubmits. The recommended client patterns:
- **Ordinary retry** → resubmit with the same **Idempotency-Key** (mechanics owned by
  0008.5/0008.11); a duplicate of an *in-flight* run resolves to the existing `run_id`,
  never a second process.
- **A run abandoned by a harness crash (sandbox intact)** → just resubmit; the controller
  fast-path self-heals the stale slot and a fresh run **resumes on the same sandbox from the
  last committed checkpoint** (0008.8) — no manual cancel needed.
- **A run abandoned by sandbox *loss*** → the session is **tombstoned** (`410`); **create a
  new session** and resubmit there (v1 has no rebuild-from-checkpoint — that is v2).
- **A genuinely concurrent live run** → the second submit gets a flat **`409`** with the
  active `run_id`; the only escape hatch is **explicit cancel-then-submit** (no
  supersede/interrupt/queue in v1). Supersede/interrupt and queuing-cancellation remain
  future work owned by this sub-FRD.

Three failure signals stay deliberately **distinct**: the `abandoned` *run state*
(**no terminal reported** — infra loss or silent harness death), the active-run **`409`** (a live run holds the slot), and an
**idempotency-key conflict** (same key, different payload). They live in different
layers and must not blur; the wire spellings are settled with 0008.11.

### Why no Durable Functions

Durable Functions (DF) is Azure's framework for **reliable multi-step workflows that run
*inside* Functions** — it checkpoints an orchestration's progress to storage and
**replays** it after a crash, then layers on automatic retries, durable timers, queues,
and fan-out/fan-in. Its power is *remembering where a workflow is* when the Functions
process itself owns the long-running work.

**The deciding question: who owns the long-running work?** Here it is **not** Functions.
The agent loop runs *detached inside the ACA Sandbox*, and its progress lives in the
the sandbox's own on-disk state (`status.json`/`result.json`). Functions is the **front desk**:
authenticate → hand the job to the sandbox → write one line in the Tables ledger. DF
would be a project manager hired to "remember the project" when the contractor doing the
work already keeps their own logbook.

**Where DF does *not* help here (why v1 says no):**

| DF strength | Why it's redundant in this design |
| --- | --- |
| Remembering in-flight state across restarts | The **sandbox** already owns it; if Functions recycles, the run keeps going and a later request or the periodic reconciler rebuilds status from the non-rotating `status.json`/`result.json` + Tables (terminal state is never replayed from the rotating journal). |
| Orchestrating multi-step work it runs | The work runs in the **sandbox**, not Functions — DF would *still* need custom activities to poll/await the sandbox, adding a layer without removing the hard part. |
| A durable state machine | The controller only does *conditional transitions + one create/resume/submit*; **Azure Tables ETag CAS** (Decision 20) already covers that. |

You would also **pay** for the unused power: a Durable backend to operate, the
"orchestrator must be deterministic" constraint, replay-based debugging, and another
failure surface — for features v1 doesn't use. DF also would not solve the genuinely
hard parts: detached-process ownership, auto-suspend/resume, user OBO, or exactly-once
external side effects.

**Where DF *would* help — the reconsider-triggers.** These are exactly the features v1
excludes; the moment any lands, reopen Decision 4:
- **Queued turns** (e.g. "your turn is queued, position 3") instead of today's flat
  `409` → DF orchestrator + durable queue.
- **Automatic retry with backoff** instead of caller-driven resubmit → DF activity retry
  policies.
- **Cross-sandbox fan-out/fan-in** or cross-session orchestration → v1 is
  one-sandbox-per-session, so there is nothing to fan out yet.
- **Multi-step compensation (saga)** if sandbox creation became a rollback-on-partial
  transaction → DF compensation.
- **Long human-in-the-loop waits** (hours awaiting an approval/callback) → DF durable
  timers.

**The one honest near-miss: Durable Entities.** A DF Entity is a durable, single-threaded
actor; modeling *one session = one entity* would give the single-writer guarantee
**natively** — the very reconciler-vs-controller race that *Single-writer safety* above
solves with ETag-CAS. We still chose against it for v1 because it forces the Durable
dependency **app-wide** for one narrow benefit, it *still* can't own the detached sandbox
process (you'd keep a periodic reaper for expiry/reaping anyway), and ETag-CAS on Tables already
delivers single-writer safety with no new dependency. It is a reasonable alternative we
rejected — which is exactly why "revisit Durable" stays a live trigger, not a closed
door.

**Bottom line.** DF earns its keep when Functions runs and must durably remember
multi-step work. Here the sandbox runs the work and remembers it, and the controller has
one handoff step — so DF adds cost, not safety. The day v1 grows queuing, retries,
fan-out, or sagas, that calculus flips.

## Alternatives considered & why the choice won

- **Durable dependency (Decision 4).** Considered: Durable required for every run;
  optional future backend; or no durable execution ever. Chosen: not required for
  v1, reconsider for cross-session orchestration/managed retry — Durable's value
  (retries/fan-out/orchestration timers & workflow waits) is exactly what v1 doesn't do. *Human.*
- **Controller coordination (Decision 21, reaffirms 4).** Considered: mandatory
  Durable; a minimal Table state machine; or no external run records. Chosen: the
  minimal no-retry Table machine, because ADC owns the live process; revisit before
  adding queues/retries/fan-out/compensation. *Agent.*
- **Expired-session cleanup (Decision 22).** Considered: a Durable eternal orchestration;
  opportunistic-only (no timer); or a minimal periodic reconciler + opportunistic fast-paths.
  Chosen for v1: **a minimal periodic reconciler** (plain timer, ≈ 1 h configurable) as the
  guaranteed backstop, **augmented by** opportunistic fast-paths (after-create sweep,
  reap-on-capacity-failure, controller fast-path) + client polling. A Durable eternal
  orchestration reintroduces the dependency Decision 4 avoids; opportunistic-only was tried
  and **rejected** — it cannot *guarantee* catching a **crashed** harness (which never journals
  or polls, so no request/create is guaranteed to arrive), and a permanently-idle app never
  fires a fast-path, so a periodic sweep is the irreducible
  floor. The relaxed cadence keeps it cheap; v2 tightens it for the mirror SLO. *Agent +
  Human.*
- **Mirror cadence (Decision 24).** Considered: on-demand only; continuous sandbox
  write; or periodic controller reconciliation. Chosen: periodic reconciliation keeping
  storage credentials out of the sandbox — continuous writes would require giving the
  sandbox storage access (violating the 0008.3 RBAC model). *Agent.* **Scope note:** the
  mirror job + two-minute p95 SLO ship in **v2** (0008.8 deferred the external Blob
  mirror, Decision 18); **v1 runs the minimal periodic reconciler (Decision 22) but no
  checkpoint-mirror reconciliation** — ACA lifecycle owns idle-suspend, the reconciler +
  fast-paths free slots and reclaim, the aggregate leans on ACA — and the cadence tightens to
  ≈once-a-minute in v2 solely to hold the mirror SLO.

## Honest critique

**Strengths.** The "ADC owns the process, the journal owns in-flight state, so the
controller stays a tiny conditional state machine" argument is coherent and is the
right reason to *avoid* Durable rather than avoiding it dogmatically. The failure
table is unusually thorough and consistently chooses **fail-closed refusal** over
destructive auto-recovery. Keeping storage credentials out of the sandbox by having
the *controller/reconciler* do the mirror is a clean privilege decision.

**Risks / assumptions.**
- **No automatic retry pushes work onto callers.** A failed run is just `failed`;
  the caller must resubmit (with an Idempotency-Key). That's simple but shifts
  reliability burden to clients, and "tools must tolerate retries" is asserted, not
  enforced.
- **A periodic reconciler adds a (small) always-on component.** v1 reinstates a minimal
  timer as the guaranteed floor — a reversal of the interim "no v1 timer" stance, forced by
  the crash-before-signal case (a crashed harness can't self-report, so only the periodic sweep
  is *guaranteed* to catch it). The cost is one always-on timer trigger, mitigated by a relaxed adaptive
  cadence (≈ 1 h default), a cheap no-op query on an idle app, and the common case being
  handled promptly by client-poll + the fast-paths. The residual cost-edge is the
  awake-compute tail of an un-restored sandbox, bounded to one *configured* cadence (≈ 1 h
  default, up to several hours if relaxed; assuming a healthy timer).
- **Hand-written state machine risk.** Small now, but every added state/edge is
  manual and untested-by-framework; the very extensions the design defers (queues,
  fan-out) are exactly where hand-rolled state machines historically rot. The
  revisit-Durable trigger must actually be honored.
- **Reconciler as single writer — addressed.** Opportunistic reconciles can run
  concurrently (two creates sweeping at once) or race the controller's inline repair;
  *Single-writer safety* above resolves this with ETag-CAS monotonic transitions +
  read-not-lock liveness (never taking the controller's short lease), so concurrent
  reconciles and a live controller converge safely without a correctness-critical distributed lock.

**Reconsider triggers.** Revisit (and reconsider Durable) if: the design adds
queued turns, cross-sandbox work, retry policies, or multi-step compensation; the
periodic reconciler (v1) or the tighter v2 mirror timer can't hold its cadence/SLO at the
100-concurrent target (warranting partitioning or a queue — the last of which reopens
Durable); or the hand-written state machine grows enough edges that correctness becomes
hard to reason about.

## Resolution status & what's still open

The original open questions have largely been resolved through cross-sibling
coordination (all cross-referenced above); this records where each landed.

- **Reconciler scale/backlog (Q1) — resolved for v1.** v1's periodic reconciler is a
  **low-frequency backstop** (≈ 1 h, adaptive) whose tick is a cheap bounded query over the
  source-of-truth table that
  no-ops on an idle app — so there is no hot loop to scale; the common case rides the
  fast-paths + client-poll. Safety never hinged on a latency SLO: reaping is
  **generation-guarded** and (in v2) **mirror-before-reap**. The scale pressure is a *v2*
  concern (the ~1-min mirror cadence at 100 concurrent — shorter interval / partitioning /
  a queue), recorded under *Reconsider triggers*.
- **Reaper role / platform auto-delete — resolved.** ACA autosuspend owns idle→suspend; the
  **reaper is the authoritative deleter** of the sandbox/snapshot in v1 (the coarse group
  `auto_delete` is only a far backstop), and it also owns **idle-policy repair, crash
  detection, external Table-record cleanup, and the `abandoned` reconciliation** it
  fundamentally can't delegate — running on the **minimal periodic reconciler + opportunistic
  fast-paths**.
  Deriving liveness from the platform was **considered and rejected** (atomicity/TOCTOU +
  can't prove a process stopped); see *Single-writer safety*.
- **Reconciler single-writer safety (Q2) — resolved & signed off.** See *Single-writer
  safety*: **pure ETag-CAS + generation-guard** (read-not-lock liveness), safe under
  concurrent reconciles (opportunistic fast-paths + the periodic sweep), no correctness-critical distributed lock; soft-yield is an optional
  optimization. Human sign-off 2026-07-23.
- **Retry/resubmit UX (Q3) — resolved.** See *No automatic retry & the resubmit
  pattern*.
- **Quota specifics (Q4) — resolved for v1 (no caps).** v1 enforces **no runtime quota
  counter**: the aggregate limit is ACA's live Sandbox-Group capacity (reap-on-capacity-
  failure + retry), and **per-owner fairness quota is deferred to v2** (a single owner can
  use full app capacity in v1; no fairness guard in multi-user apps — accepted, flagged to
  0008.2). The Table stays for its non-quota roles (ownership / one-active-run / status /
  generation).
- **Retention model & idle durations — resolved (hybrid).** Idle-based, clock from
  terminal (ratified); no absolute creation-time TTL. v1 defaults **~5 min auto-suspend /
  ~24 h idle reclaim**. **Hybrid**: group lifecycle policy = customer-IaC default; the

- **`410 Gone` vs. rebuild (Q5) — resolved for v1.** v1: status durable once recorded (controller / request / reconciler pass; Tables), content
